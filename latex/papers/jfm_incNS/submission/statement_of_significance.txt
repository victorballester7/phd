Experiments show that sufficiently large surface gaps move boundary-layer transition onto the gap itself, but the mechanism has remained unclear. Using direct numerical simulation and global and local stability analysis, we show that a gap either amplifies incoming Tollmien-Schlichting waves or loses stability to two- or three-dimensional modes. Neither instability alone causes breakdown at the gap. Transition occurs at the gap only when both are present, and the upper envelope of the two neutral curves reproduces the measured bypass boundary. This gives a calibration-free physical criterion for gap tolerances on laminar-flow surfaces.
